Executive Summary

A wireless temperature sensor that reports every 5 minutes runs for more than a year on two AA cells. Reporting every minute cuts that to three months, and the radio is the reason.

Recommendation

Ship with the 5-minute interval as the default. Offer 1 minute as an option with a warning about battery life, and test change-based reporting next.

1Result

Three identical sensors ran for 30 days, each at its own reporting interval. Figure 1 shows the capacity left in the cells, measured once a day. After 30 days the 1-minute sensor had 68% left; the other two had lost less than a tenth.

1.1Where the current goes

Each report costs one radio burst of 18 mA. Between bursts the sensor sleeps at 35 µA and wakes briefly to sample. Figure 2 splits the average current into those three consumers. Sleep and sampling barely change with the interval; the radio share falls from 90% to 52%.

2What to try next

Two changes could extend battery life without losing the detail a 1-minute interval gives.

Report on change

  • Send only when the temperature moved more than 0.2 °C.
  • Keep a heartbeat every 15 minutes.
  • Expected: close to the 15-minute result in a stable room.

Lower transmit power

  • Sensors within 10 m of the gateway do not need full power.
  • Cuts the burst from 18 mA to about 11 mA.
  • Needs a link-quality check before each step down.

2.1Method

  • Three identical sensors, one per interval, on fresh cells from the same batch.
  • Current measured with a source meter at 10 kHz, averaged per hour.
  • Remaining capacity from a daily discharge-curve reading at rest.
  • Projected life uses 2,500 mAh of usable capacity per pair of cells.
